Three copies of one object side by side in a row, for a pixel art video game in the 16-bit console style of the early 1990s, evenly spaced with wide flat pure magenta space between them. The object is a small round hover platform that one person stands on, on a space station: a low circular metal craft like a thick disc, seen from the front and slightly above so its flat top is a wide shallow oval. Its top is a non-slip steel deck plate with a ring of small cyan running lights round its edge; its side is a chunky gunmetal rim with bolts and a thin orange stripe; and bolted underneath are three small rocket thrusters, each firing a bright blue jet of flame straight down that holds the craft up: white-hot at the nozzle, then cyan, then deep blue at the tip. The thrusters and jets match those under the barrel in @barrel. Nothing stands on the platform. The craft is about three times as wide as it is tall, not counting the jets. The three copies are exactly the same craft in exactly the same place and size; only the blue jets differ: in the first they are short, in the second medium, in the third long, as three frames of a flickering flame. Fine small pixels, smooth painted shading in four or five close tones, clean dark outlines, small sharp highlights, light from the upper left. No shadow, no floor, no people, no text.
